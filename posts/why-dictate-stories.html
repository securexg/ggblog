Why I Stopped Fighting Text Editors and Started Dictating Visual Stories

Most blogging platforms force a choice between clean writing and rich visuals. Owning a simple HTML folder gives you both.

I
Inklog Editorial
Dictated & published on Cloudflare Pages

When you only write occasionally, the hardest part of publishing is rarely the idea itself. It is the friction between having a thought and getting it onto a page that actually looks good when someone opens the link.

Hosted editors like Medium are great for plain paragraphs, right up until you want a structured comparison table, custom visual framing, or full control over how images sit on the screen. Paste raw HTML there and it breaks. Paste a table and it flattens.

Every story lives as a plain HTML file in one folder, backed up to GitHub and served globally on Cloudflare Pages. No database, no CMS updates, and no lock-in.

Speak the draft, shape the layout

Instead of staring at a blinking cursor, it is much faster to talk through the story out loud. Once the ideas are out, turning that transcript into clear sections, a readable comparison table, and well-placed photography takes minutes rather than hours.

Publishing Setup Tables & Custom Layout Storage & Ownership Reader Experience
Medium No raw HTML or native tables Zero setup, stored on Medium Clean, but gated by member popups
Notion Native tables, HTML only in framed box Zero setup, stored in workspace Great for internal docs, feels like a wiki
GitHub + Cloudflare Pages Full HTML, CSS, tables, and visuals One local folder, backed up forever Fast, ad-free, clean short URL

What comes next

This first post is a working baseline. Whenever there is a new story to share, all it takes is dictating the rough version, dropping in the right visuals, and pushing one update so the link goes live.
