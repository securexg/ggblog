Simple Things Matter Most: Strength, Meditation, and Cutting Out Reels

I stopped using Instagram for repeated content. Everything worth keeping comes down to three simple truths: build muscle, calm the mind, and protect your attention.

Talking about simple things matters the most. That is why I stopped using Instagram for repeated, recycled content. People overcomplicate everything, when the real fundamentals have not changed.

1. Health: Strength Training Is Non-Negotiable

Strength training is unavoidable for long-term health, regardless of age or gender. Cardio and walking are good, but they cannot replace muscle-building resistance training.

If you can, go to a nearby gym instead of relying on dumbbells at home. A gym gives you energy, connection, and consistency.

  • Do: Lift weights a few days a week at a nearby gym.
  • Don't: Treat walking or an hour of cardio as a replacement for strength training.

2. Diet: 80% Food, Clear Out Protein Myths

Fitness is roughly 80% diet and 20% exercise. Drink more water, cut excess carbs, and keep meals minimal.

Since I am a vegetarian, here are the highest vegetarian protein sources that actually work:

  • Pure whey protein (cleanest and easiest way to hit daily targets)
  • Soya chunks (widely available in India and very high in protein)
  • Roti protein mix (available in the market to boost everyday meals)
  • Rajma and dal (have protein, but very, very minimal)

If you eat eggs, they are a good, cheap source of protein. I do not take them because I am vegetarian, but they are worth noting. Outside food is packed with sugar and cheap oils; enjoy cold drinks, pizza, chips, momos, or Maggi occasionally for fun, not as daily meals.

Common Protein Myths vs. Facts
Roti & Rice: Primarily carbs, not protein. Keep portions minimal and pair with real protein.
Sattu & Oats: Carb-heavy with moderate protein. Do not rely on them as your main protein source.
Peanut Butter: Mostly fats and calories. Use sparingly; add whey or sorted veg protein.

3. The Mind: Meditation Is the Only Way Out

No one can avoid stress from relationships, finances, or health, because no life is perfect. When the mind gets worn down, meditation is the only real way out.

Clear the glitter out of empty outer rituals and focus on your breath and inner self. Personally, I practice meditation through courses inspired by Gurudev Sri Sri Ravi Shankar. Pick one path and practice it a few days a week.

4. Protect Both: Block Short-Form Reels

Short-form reels are engineered to hook you and steal hours from your day. I use a free Android app called ScrollLess to block short videos and set timers (similar blockers exist on iOS).

  • Do: Use a short-video blocker so your attention is protected by default.
  • Don't: Rely on willpower alone against algorithmic feeds.

Aim for 70%, Not Perfection

You cannot follow everything 100%. Aim for 60% to 80% consistency. Some days you will eat junk food or miss meditation, and that is normal. Just stay aware of the basics: the mind is cleaned by meditation, and the body is built by strength and protein.

Scientific References
Strength Training: 90 to 120 min/week lowers all-cause mortality by 13% and preserves muscle and bone density. Harvard / BJSM (PubMed)
Added Sugars: WHO recommends keeping free sugars below 10% of daily energy intake (ideally under 5%). World Health Organization
Meditation: Mindfulness and meditation reduce psychological stress and anxiety. NIH / NCCIH
Short-Form Video: Problematic short-video use is linked to attentional fragmentation. PubMed Central (NIH)
