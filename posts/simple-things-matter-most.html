Simple Things Matter Most: Strength, Meditation, and Cutting Out Reels

I stopped using Instagram for repeated content. Everything worth keeping comes down to three simple truths: build muscle, calm the mind, and protect your attention.

Talking about simple things matters the most. That is why I stopped using Instagram for repeated, recycled content. People try to overcomplicate everything, when the real fundamentals of a good life have not changed.

My basic philosophy comes down to three things: health, the mind, and protecting your attention. And just as important as knowing what to do is knowing what not to do.

1. Health: Strength Training Is Non-Negotiable

Health is the most important part of your life, and strength training is unavoidable. Muscle is something you need for the long term, and it is non-negotiable for any gender or any age group. It cannot be replaced with anything at all.

Cardio and walking are not bad, but cardio cannot replace strength training. When people claim that walking is the best exercise or just do cardio for an hour every day, I genuinely feel pity for them. I have said this so many times, yet because of unscientific notions and old-age misconceptions, many people still avoid lifting weights. You cannot control anyone else; that is their choice. But I am happy keeping to the simplest truth: strength training is essential.

And if you can, go to a nearby gym instead of relying on dumbbells at home. Dumbbells at home rarely bring the same motivation. In a gym, you feel the energy and the connection around you, and that environment keeps you showing up.

  • Do: Lift weights a few days a week at a nearby gym to build long-term muscle.
  • Don't: Treat walking or an hour of cardio as a replacement for strength training.

2. Diet: 80% Food, Clear Out the Protein Myths

Fitness is roughly 80% diet and 20% exercise. We can only exercise for 30 to 40 minutes, or at most an hour a day, but diet is something you take care of all day long. Drink more water, cut out excess carbs, and eat minimal, healthy meals.

Whether you are vegetarian or non-vegetarian, you need adequate protein. If you are vegetarian, you have to find sorted protein sources and include things like whey protein. Too many myths are floating around about what counts as protein, and they need to be cleared out:

What People Think Is Protein What It Actually Is What to Do Instead
Roti & Rice Carbs, not protein Keep portions minimal; pair with real protein
Sattu Not a complete protein source Do not rely on it as your main protein
Oats Primarily carbs Treat as carbs, not a protein meal
Peanut Butter Mostly fats and calories Use sparingly; add whey or sorted veg protein

If you go anywhere outside, everything is filled with sugar and cheap oils, because those are the cheapest ingredients to pack behind colorful labels. I am not saying you can never have cold drinks, pizzas, chips, momos, or Maggi. You can have them, just in the right proportion, knowing they are for fun and not your ultimate healthy meal.

  • Do: Prioritize adequate protein (including whey if needed), drink more water, and keep meals minimal.
  • Don't: Mistake roti, rice, sattu, oats, or peanut butter for high-protein foods, or treat packaged sugar-and-oil snacks as daily meals.

3. The Mind: Meditation Is the Only Way Out

A very simple truth about the body is that it runs on four things: sleep, diet, stress management, and exercise. Coming to stress, which is the most important part, I want to be very clear: no one in this world can avoid stress. It can come from relationships, finances (which is often the most prominent one), health, or anything else, because no one's life is perfect and it can never be perfect.

Because of that, the mind gets damaged a lot. And if there is anything that can truly help the mind, it is only meditation. Once you go beyond the intellect, only then can you understand yourself. That is the only real, scientific way out.

Ancient traditions have been telling us since time immemorial to go deep inside ourselves. Yet even in India, people often focus too much on outer rituals and idols, damaging the environment and polluting rivers, while ignoring their own mind, breath, and meditation.

We have to clear the glitter out and focus on what is actually important. When you meditate and connect to your inner self, you connect to the universe and God directly. You cannot completely eliminate stress, but meditation helps you navigate it, keep your mind calmer, and find better solutions. Personally, I practice meditation through courses inspired by Gurudev Sri Sri Ravi Shankar. There are many paths available across the world and the internet; pick one path and do it.

  • Do: Meditate a few days a week (alternating with strength training) to keep your mind calm and clear.
  • Don't: Get lost in empty outer rituals while ignoring your breath and inner state.

4. Protect Both: Block Short-Form Reels

There is one modern habit that damages both your body and your mind: short-form reels. All of them are strongly engineered to keep you hooked, and there is no way you can naturally bypass them on willpower alone. They are structured to destroy your focus and steal hours out of your day.

I use a free Android app called ScrollLess to block short videos and set timers, and there are similar blockers available on iOS too. It is not a foolproof system, but you still need to set blockers and keep reminding yourself again and again that these short videos are not the answer.

  • Do: Install a short-video blocker or timer so your attention is protected by default.
  • Don't: Rely on willpower alone against algorithms designed to keep you scrolling.

Aim for 70%, Not Perfection

You do not have to be super strict about everything. Obviously it is not possible to follow everything 100%. Aim for 60%, 70%, or 80%. Sometimes you will get hooked on things, eat junk food, or miss meditation, and your mind will feel spoiled. That is completely normal.

What matters is staying aware of the basic, raw scientific truth: the mind is cleaned by meditation, and the body is built by strength and protein. Keep coming back to the simple things.
