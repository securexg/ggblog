From Ghosted by Editors to 2 AM AI Cuts: My Podcast Video Editing Journey

We tried freelance editors and an established studio for my wife's podcast. After months of delays and blocked communication, AI video tools gave us our control back.

My wife started a podcast a few months back, and like most creators, we began by looking for video editors. Our first editor was paid at market rate. There were delays, but he did good work until he eventually stopped working altogether.

After that, we tried other editors. One over-promised and barely executed. Then we tied up with an established studio, thinking they would bring reliability. Instead, they were doing bulk work, and our episodes got mediocre edits with constant communication issues.

The Three Problems That Broke Our Workflow

Paying for very expensive editors was not realistic when the podcast was not yet generating huge revenue. We needed a balanced way, but the traditional options kept hitting the same walls:

  • Communication and time lag: Simple revisions took days of waiting.
  • Not meeting expectations: Instructions were ignored even after full or advance payments.
  • Complete communication blocks: At one point, we were stuck with our own footage and even had to go physically to collect our data because they stopped replying.

It became an extreme deadlock. Instagram and YouTube uploads for my wife's podcast were delayed, clients were waiting, and payments were held up.

Discovering Cardboard AI and Settling on Ponder AI

At first, I tried editing with general coding agents like Command Code and Antigravity, and even tested Claude for video scripts. They could handle basic tasks, but they lacked the creative edge, so we still thought we needed a human editor.

Then I tried a startup called Cardboard AI on their cloud plan just as a test. To my shock, what it built in 15 minutes was genuinely amazing. That moment changed my mind, and I started studying AI video editing tools deeply.

Eventually, I settled on Ponder AI, which is built by a filmmaker, has its own memory, and connects directly to my command bar.

The Math: ₹4,000 a Month and Full Control

Right now, my setup costs $20 for Claude Code and $29 for Ponder AI (around ₹4,000 in total). Ponder AI's plan gives me 10 hours of footage, and reels or trailers are only a minute each. Even with Claude Code's 5-hour usage window, I can easily put together 2 to 3 videos in a session.

Why This Shift Is Happening

It is not just about saving money. The real problem we faced was lack of responsibility: people taking payment and then refusing to do the basic work they agreed to.

For mediocre, repetitive edits, AI tools already handle the job easily. For deeper creative work, you still need to sit down and give thoughtful instructions, and smart creators will learn how to do that. I feel blessed to learn these tools, because it has completely relieved my mind knowing I can edit my wife's podcast videos anytime.
